`status: proposed`**, awaiting a human ruling; and **a slice carrying `deferred_from` that is not

**A row states what is owed, never that anything is correct.** A `proposed` row leaves when the human
accepts or rejects the ADR — in the moment, or by standing delegation; an *agreed and unbuilt* one
when its slice reaches `done`. A merge moves neither. What is live in `main` while a remedy is
outstanding is behaviour, recorded where the behaviour is described.

### The cost of the literal AC-6 ruling — slice 01

A `src/domain` module imports **nothing at all**, its siblings included (§5.2). These four entries are
the standing price of that ratified decision. **None is an argument for revisiting it.**

| id | The cost |
|---|---|
| **D-01-1** | The composition order is written out by a use case in `src/application` rather than made uncallable in any other sequence by branded types. Still correct, and correct because someone wrote it correctly |
| **D-01-2** | Both inter-module handoffs take a bare `number`, so minutes passed where milliseconds are expected compiles and yields a plausible interval rather than a crash. The brands catch that *inside* a module and never between them, which is the boundary they were introduced for. **Cashed in**: ADR-0014 places `8_640_000_000_000_000` in two domain files with no mechanism to share it, so one concept has two homes and only review and a property test hold them equal |
| **D-01-3** | `malformed-interval` is an extra branch and verdict variant, existing only because the `Interval` type cannot cross the boundary carrying *"ordered, and from the same interval"*. Fail-closed and directly testable, so a branch to cover rather than a risk to carry |
| **D-01-4** | The three-file split has lost one of its two justifications — the three types no longer compose — and now rests on ambiguity containment alone. *"The scan requires it"* is thinner than *"the types require it"*: a real weakening, not a fatal one, and the reason a later slice should not simply inline `interval.ts` |

### What the slice-01 scans do not catch

`duration-arithmetic` (QS-12) defines its marker as a *concept* — any conversion between minutes or
seconds and milliseconds outside `src/domain/duration.ts` — against an enumerated, deliberately open
spelling set. What it misses is **irreducible for a text scan**, not a promise of a cleverer regex:

| Escape | Example | Status |
|---|---|---|
| exponent notation | `6e4`, `60 * 1e3` | gap |
| a conversion routed through an imported named constant | `import { MS_PER_MINUTE } …` | gap |
| any computed form | `Math.pow(10, 3)` | gap |
| a quantity whose name says neither minutes nor seconds | `elapsed * 1000` | **deliberate** — scoping by the quantity's name keeps `kilobytes * 1000` out; widening it trades a gap for false positives |

The first three are gaps, not licences: a spelling not listed is a finding to raise.
`outside-in-tests-do-not-import-src` (QS-10) has the matching hole one level up, described in §8.5:
relative `../src/` references are caught, computed forms are not, and the residue is review.

### What the domain tests still do not evidence

QS-9 and QS-12 are enforced by committed tests, which is not the same as fully evidenced:

- **QS-9 examines one zone and one year**, `Europe/London` in 2026. A southern-hemisphere transition, a
  non-whole-hour offset and a zone whose rules changed mid-year are untested; the rule is zone-generic
  by construction, not by evidence.
- **The `Intl` global is an ICU dependency the purity rule cannot see.** `domain-is-pure` is satisfied
  because there is no module specifier to record, but a Node build with small-icu would change
  `withinOpeningHours`'s answers without changing a line of `src/`. TC-10's version pin is what stands
  there, and it is not aimed at this.
- **QS-12's corpus is small.** `src/**/*.ts` is twenty-one files, three of them the domain, so a marker
  matching in exactly one file is a stronger claim than it was at slice 01 and still not a strong one.

### The cost of ADR-0018's locks — slice 02

Two advisory locks now precede every `INSERT` on the booking path (§6.1). They are what makes the
`409` reachable at all, and they are not free.

| id | The cost |
|---|---|
| **R-02-2** | **ADR-0016's argument is weaker after ADR-0018 than before it, and the ADR says so in its own Consequences.** Inside a per-resource lock a reintroduced check-then-act would be *correct*, not merely harmless — so the reading that bounded the damage, *"the exclusion constraint makes a check redundant"*, no longer bounds it. ## 11.2 Known risks

Ordered by the cost of being wrong, not by likelihood.

### R-1 · The write-throughput ceiling bought with goal 1

§1.2 ranks integrity first and performance last with the cost stated, and this is the cost. Two limits
are confused. **Per contended key**, conflicting inserts serialise — since ADR-0018 on the advisory
lock rather than the constraint, at three round trips per attempt (§6.1) — but only one can succeed,
so what it caps is how fast losers are told *no*; non-conflicting inserts do not interact.
**In aggregate**, every insert maintains two partial GiST indexes, materially costlier than a btree:
low thousands of inserts per second on modest hardware. Against §1.1's load profile that is two orders
of magnitude of headroom in aggregate and about five on the contended path.

**Revisit when** sustained bookings exceed a few hundred per second, or `appointment` passes
single-digit millions of live rows and GiST maintenance shows in write latency. **The first move is
partitioning by `dealership_id`**, which works because of A-9: an exclusion constraint cannot span
partitions and need not, an appointment never spanning two dealerships.

### R-2 · A capacity-*n* resource would need a different mechanism (A-2)

An exclusion constraint expresses capacity **one**, exactly, so a technician overseeing two jobs or a
bay taking two small vehicles makes the mechanism the wrong shape and no tuning fixes it. The cheapest
route keeps the mechanism and changes the model: give the resource *n* numbered slots, each a
capacity-one resource, so only candidate generation changes. A counting constraint (which PostgreSQL
has no declarative form of), and `SERIALIZABLE` plus an application-side count, both move correctness
back into code against `CLAUDE.md` §2.1.

### R-3 to R-6 · Four couplings that nothing structural enforces

| id | The coupling | What holds it, and what does not |
|---|---|---|
| R-3 | **The constraint names are behaviour, not documentation.** ADR-0009 prunes from `err.constraint` and §8.4 labels `booking_conflicts_total{resource}` from it | Renaming `no_bay_overlap` degrades the retry loop to a multiplicative bound and mislabels the metric — without failing to compile and without looking wrong in a single-threaded test. QS-1 and QS-2 assert the names; nothing else does |
| R-4 | **The attempt cap refuses while capacity exists, and this row said the opposite until slice 04 (D-04-1).** ADR-0004 accepted a residual refusal as a liveness guard; ADR-0009 set the cap at 16 | It said reaching 16 needs sixteen resources taken *from under one request while it loops*. It needs sixteen **conflicts**, and a merely busy resource supplies one with no concurrency at all. ADR-0009 set the cap **below the bound its own Bound-2 paragraph computed** — `\|bays\| + \|technicians\| − 1`, which at §1.1 scale exceeds 16 with or without an availability filter. So *"a non-zero `capped` counter means the cap is wrong"* is already false: it is expected. Slice 09's **AC-13** — one `INSERT` on a 5-bay, 20-technician, 500-appointment fixture, bound 24 — is the sibling that cannot pass until this closes. Two remedies, neither chosen: the pre-filter after slice 08's QS-8, or a cap above the bound. The number is ADR-0009's and human-decided |
| D-04-2 | **ADR-0020's cap placement is a rule about where a `return` goes**, stated only by `tsc` | It objects once someone moves the cap *and* refuses from outside the arm. No test can help: AC-4 makes a refusal minted in the arm and one cast outside it render identically |
| R-5 | **The exclusion constraint's range expression and the availability query's are one idea in two files** | §4.2 records why a shared `IMMUTABLE` SQL function cannot hold them together, so QS-8 is load-bearing. Weaken it and the risk is unmitigated with no other signal |
| R-6 | **The `Database` interface can drift from the migrations.** ADR-0006 keeps schema types in `schema.ts` and the schema in `.sql` | Nothing, until a CI check regenerates from a migrated database and fails on a diff. Until then a migration merged without a matching type edit compiles and is wrong |

| R-7b | `src/http` may import `src/domain`, and the rule is not "types only", so policy in a route handler would pass `dependency-cruiser` | QS-12 catches the three ambiguities that matter; the rest is review |

### R-8 · Three things CI is *said* to enforce and does not

ADR-0010 founds the pipeline (§7.4). The failure mode of an unenforced enforcement claim is that
everyone stops checking by hand, so the open claims are listed rather than left implicit.

| Claimed | Claimed by | State today |
|---|---|---|
| The diagram scripts `self_check.py` and `verify-geometry.py` run in CI | METHODOLOGY §4 | **Cannot run** — they live in a plugin cache outside the repository and nothing vendors them. CI checks the honest subset: every `.html` has a committed `.svg`, and every diagram link resolves |
| Link integrity and ADR existence are enforced | METHODOLOGY §4 | No tool. CI checks diagram links only; every other relative link is unchecked |
| `QS-*` names a real test, or CI fails | METHODOLOGY §4, §10.2 | No tool, and no longer blocked now that `tests/` exists. The traceability chain's last link is a plain gap, and the oldest unpaid item here |

Not a gap but worth knowing: **`log:audit` cannot run in CI** — its ground truth is subagent
transcripts that exist only on the maintainer's machine. It is a gate-time command; CI substitutes the
two structural checks it can make, append-only and schema-valid.

### R-12 · The mutation gate's failure mode is silence, and it is held by a workaround

The tool producing §8.5's score has a demonstrated mode in which it reports survivors it never tested;
§8.5 carries the measurement, the workaround and the re-measurement recipe. **The residual risk is that
it was caught by a low score, which is luck**: a broken runner producing 0.81 against a 0.75 threshold
satisfies every check `slice:check` makes, and nothing fails if someone sets `testRunner: 'vitest'`
back. The remedy is the one that closed the same shape at §5.3 and §7.2 — put the assertion **inside
the thing that produces the pass**, here a `mutation.json` check on `testsCompleted`. Until then the
mitigation is a reviewer reading the survivor list, which is real and is not a mechanism.

### R-9 · `db:migrate` does not conform to ADR-0007, and the built artifact cannot migrate

Two items, recorded together because **the second is a trap laid for whoever pays the first**.

**R-9a — the conformance drift.** [ADR-0007](../adr/0007-node-pg-migrate-with-sql-files.md)'s Decision
states that the runner is invoked *"programmatically … both by `npm run db:migrate` … and by the
Testcontainers fixture"*. `package.json` invokes the CLI binary. The observable consequence is the
`--single-transaction` default (§7.2): a malformed migration rolls everything back under `db:migrate`
and leaves earlier files committed under the test harness. This is debt of an unusual kind — not code
falling short of a document, but **an accepted, immutable decision the code silently stopped
implementing**.

> **Recommendation, so it is not re-argued: conform `db:migrate`; do not supersede ADR-0007.**
> Legitimising an unargued drift is the worse precedent, and the ADR's requirement — in-process, no
> shelling out to a binary that may not be on the path — is right.

**R-9b — `dist/persistence/` holds no migrations.** `tsc` emits only what it compiles, so `npm run
build` copies no `.sql` and the built artifact cannot migrate itself. Inert today, because both
migration paths read the working tree and `dist/main.js` never migrates; real the first time the
service is packaged. **The coupling is the point** — a programmatic `db:migrate` must resolve its
migrations directory from a location the build populates, or the fix for R-9a ships broken.

### R-11 · Assertions that would survive their own subject being deleted

Two items sharing a shape: **a specification exists, the thing it specifies works, and nothing would
notice if it stopped.**

**R-11a — four reference-table constraints.** `opening_hours.day_of_week BETWEEN 0 AND 6`,
`opening_hours.closes_at > opens_at`, `service_type.duration_minutes > 0` and `vehicle.vin UNIQUE` are
specified in §8.1 and asserted by nothing. Measured live: all four exist and all four fire (`23514`
×3, `23505`), and dropping any one leaves the suite green. Every other reference-table constraint is
self-enforcing, the `UNIQUE (id, dealership_id)` pairs and `technician_qualification`'s primary key
being foreign-key targets whose removal fails migration `0003`. The remedy is **not** extending case 0
across nine tables — the first step back toward the whole-schema snapshot slice 00 rejected as brittle
— but **asserting them where the code that relies on them lands**.

**R-11b — `appointment_technician_in_dealership` is proven to exist and not to fire.** Six of the seven
`appointment` constraints have a case that provokes them; the seventh has only case 0. Measured:
booking a D1 technician under D2 *is* rejected `23503` on it. Drop it and case 0's set-equality is the
only assertion that fails; mis-key it and case 0's definition-equality is. **The technician half of
A-9 rides on a catalogue assertion alone**, where the bay half has a behavioural one.

### R-10 · `updated_at` is maintained by the writer, and nothing enforces it

`appointment.updated_at` has `DEFAULT now()` and no trigger. ADR-0003's move is an `UPDATE`, so unless
every such statement sets `updated_at = now()` the column is silently wrong from slice 05. No trigger,
deliberately: §2.1's discipline is that the database holds the **invariant** and the application the
convenience. The obligation sits on `appointmentRepository.ts` and no test asserts it.

## 11.3 What production would additionally require

Scope cut deliberately is judgement; cut silently it is a gap. Every item traces to a §3.3 exclusion
marked **†** or a Gate A ruling.

| Missing | Consequence today | What adding it costs |
|---|---|---|
| **Authentication and authorisation** (ADR-0002, GC-2) | **Unsafe on any reachable network**: anyone who can reach the port can book, read and cancel on any customer's behalf | Not additive. Ownership moves from validation into a security boundary, `422` becomes `403`, the body must stop distinguishing "not yours" from "does not exist", and every test asserting the current shape is revised |
| **Technician shifts, holidays, absence** (ADR-0001) | A technician is bookable whenever the dealership is open, day off included | The expensive one. Per-resource, time-varying availability is a *second* class of rule beside the database-enforced one, needing its own mechanism, concurrency story and quality scenario — or it is the loophole §2.1 exists to close |
| **Appointment history and audit** (ADR-0003) | "Who cancelled this, and when?" is unanswerable; no actor is on the record | An append-only history table beside `appointment`. Additive |
| **DMS event publication** (§3.1.2) | Nothing downstream learns an appointment exists | An outbox row written in the same statement as the appointment, which this design supports well because the write is already one statement |
| **High availability, backup, recovery** (§3.3) | One container. A lost volume is a lost schedule | Ordinary PostgreSQL operations. The application is stateless, so several instances need no coordination (§7.1) |
| **Vehicle-dependent durations (A-1), buffers (A-4), search-style booking (A-5)** | The three most likely real-world corrections | The first two are one domain function plus one migration each, by construction (ADR-0008, QS-12). A-5 turns the advisory availability read into something that drives allocation, and is materially larger |
| **Reference-data management** (A-7) | Bays, technicians and opening hours change only by migration | Conventional CRUD, no interesting risk, spending the review attention OC-3 names as scarce |
| **Waitlists, overbooking, priority jobs** (§3.3) | No scheduling policy beyond first-come-first-served | Policy is where a real scheduler earns its keep and cannot be done convincingly without a real dealership's data. ADR-0009's Order-D is the first honest step |
| **GDPR-grade PII handling** (§3.3) | Customer names are stored; logs carry ids only (§8.4), a mitigation rather than a policy | Retention, subject access and erasure — and erasure interacts with the history table above |
